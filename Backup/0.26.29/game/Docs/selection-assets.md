# Selection artwork and modes

Selection uses separate portrait, name and disc artwork from the supplied Select/WEB exports, not character SFF 9000,1. Portraits retain their exported canvas coordinates and are translated to system.def p1.face.offset / p2.face.offset; name artwork is centered on each player anchor. The disc artwork retains its supplied placement on the central globe.

Character images live in Char/<character>/Select; stage titles in Stage/<stage>/Select; new shared buttons in Data/Select/Buttons. The previous 0930-series icons are no longer loaded. Transparent margins are cropped with original positions recorded in Data/Select/selection-assets.json. The exporter uses WEBP quality 90 and keeps the original source PNGs unchanged.

Hardware/command buttons use 90% of their previous 0.75 display scale (0.675) and share a horizontal centerline. All buttons shrink to 90% while pressed, restoring on release/cancel. Hardware cycles 3DO → SNES → Saturn → 3DO; command cycles Normal → Auto → Normal. Confirm toggles the player's lock and 50% grayscale state; pressing it again unlocks character and mode choices. Touch users may choose character/hardware/command in any order, tapping the left/right portraits to target the character grid without visible P1/P2 labels. The globe grid remains clickable without rectangle outlines. The central Next button is removed; the second VS/Watch confirmation proceeds directly to stage selection. Training retains its Next button and only requires P1. Names occupy the former confirmation row; Confirm is at the bottom. EXIT at upper left returns directly to the title; keyboard B/Y still steps back. Stage titles alternate every four ticks. Artwork is decoded before selection becomes interactive.

Keyboard order: P1 character → hardware → command, then P2 character → hardware → command in VS/Watch, then stage. Training proceeds from P1 command to stage. A/X (A/Z on the default keyboard; Numpad1/0 for P2) confirms; B/Y (S/X; Numpad2/Decimal) returns one step. Enter/Escape remain aliases. All arrows navigate characters; only up/down changes hardware/command. Both players' hardware and command choices carry into combat, scoring and subsequent round resets.

All 13 supplied character names are included, including Super Moon and Tuxedo Mask. Only Venus and Training are playable until additional fighters and stages are implemented. Stage selection starts at Random, followed by the available stages (currently Training Room). Random resolves to an available stage on confirmation, never an unavailable stage. Left/right or touching the title cycles choices; A/X/Enter or Next confirms; B/Y/Escape steps back. The stage title occupies the upper center row at y≈610, with Next below at y=665.

Large portraits are separate textured planes within the 3D selection renderer, composited after the curtains and before the globe. Their exported screen positions and fitted viewport clipping are preserved. Globe render order is explicitly after portraits, including its fill and grid meshes; character discs, names and controls remain separate foreground overlays. Portrait textures can be updated independently for future animation without placing portraits over the globe menu.

The portrait/name/disc canvas is clipped to the fitted 1280×720 camera frame, preserving black letterbox/pillarbox areas. Falling light horizontal positions use uniformly distributed screen coordinates rather than clamping out-of-view world positions onto the two sides. Their 20–60px camera-bottom landing inset remains unchanged.

The selection background now uses Stage/Select/Select.glb.gz, converted from Select v1.glb with WEBP textures (maximum 2048 pixels). Its three original animation clips are preserved. Sphere.004 receives a cyan inner rim and soft outer halo; seven rear glare rays gently vary in intensity and direction. Ninety-six deterministic light particles descend and flare near the lower edge. Effects are additive shaders rather than full-screen bloom, so portraits and text are not blurred. Existing disc artwork remains a separate overlay. Animation stops while the selection page is hidden, and the static background remains the fallback if the 3D model cannot load.

The original Blender file is not modified. Rebuild the web model with Tools/export_select_stage.py. The camera preset named Original IKEMEN Camera corresponds to Blender location (0, -7.2, 1.6), XYZ Euler rotation (90, 0, 0) degrees: native glTF position (0, 1.6, 7.2), facing -Z, vertical FOV 30 degrees. This selection model is not given the title model's separate scale/offset transform. Disc artwork and selection hitboxes are resized together for the new camera framing; portrait/name anchors are unchanged.

The 3D animation, glow, beams and particles advance at 30 Hz with unchanged playback duration. Fractional time accumulates between updates rather than slowing animation by dropping elapsed time. Leaving selection stops animation and clears fractional timing.

Airborne particles have a dedicated soft halo pass sharing their core positions. Their landing centers are projected 20–60 CSS pixels above the actual camera viewport lower edge, using the resized renderer height rather than world-space floor coordinates or a fixed 720-pixel height. Horizontal centers are constrained inside the viewport. All particles stop before fading; eight-point star cores retain their size, round cores expand, and all halos expand and intensify. Beam shafts originate behind the sphere center with shorter fading tails. Beams brighten over 6 ticks, fade completely over 36 ticks, then remain invisible for a seeded random 90–180 ticks. Reflection stars brighten over 8 ticks, hold full light for a random 60–90 ticks, fade completely over 16 ticks, and hold transparent for a random 90–180 ticks. The upper-right star remains 20 logical screen pixels left. Existing inner/outer sphere glow and other star sizes remain unchanged.

The updated GLB preserves the longer curtain animations and removes its old sky. Stage/Select/background.webp supplies the new starfield, converted from Select/WEB/BG.jpg at quality 88. Animation remains 30 FPS with the original IKEMEN camera. The first logo letter T shifts 20 logical pixels right without changing animation timing.

Particle size uses a 2x multiplier in both airborne and landing phases, with an additional deterministic size distribution. One third are round glows and two thirds are slender eight-point stars. Rear glare beams now use narrow, long shafts with independent intensity and a visible +/-0.24-radian angular sweep. Three fixed reflection-star positions only pulse in brightness: upper-left surface, upper-right behind the sphere, lower-right surface. The two surface stars are composited after the separate character disc artwork and before large portraits, so they remain visible without covering player portraits. Inner/outer glow parameters remain unchanged. Effect parameters are in Stage/Select/select-stage.js.

Run Tools/export_selection_assets.py with Pillow to rebuild, and node Tools/check-selection.cjs to validate navigation, mode payloads, rendering and asset paths. Tools/check-selection-browser.cjs also validates the loaded 3D effects, animation, hidden-page pause and absence of shader errors; it uses a local Three.js r180 test mirror under outputs/three-r180 to avoid restricted external browser requests.
